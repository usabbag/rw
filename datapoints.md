## Core Temperature Data
- **Current temperature** (today)
- **Yesterday's temperature** (at the same time)
- **Temperature range** for today (high/low)
- **Temperature range** for yesterday (for comparison)

## Critical Perception Modifiers
- **Wind speed and direction** (both days) - The prompt emphasizes this heavily as wind "makes temperatures feel much colder"
- **Humidity levels** (both days) - Affects how temperatures actually feel to humans
- **"Feels like" temperature** - The calculated perceived temperature combining actual temp, wind, and humidity

## Weather Conditions
- **Sky conditions** (sunny/partly cloudy/overcast/rainy) for both days
- **Precipitation** status and probability
- **UV index** (for sunny days)

## Time-Based Data
- **Morning vs afternoon conditions** - The prompt notes these can differ significantly
- **Hourly forecast** for today (to advise on layering strategies)
- **Time of sunset** (affects evening temperature drops)

## Optional but Valuable
- **Pressure changes** (can indicate incoming weather shifts)
- **Visibility** (fog/mist affects clothing choices)
- **Dew point** (better indicator of comfort than humidity alone)

The prompt specifically emphasizes that the AI needs to understand not just the numbers, but how weather differences "actually feel to humans." This means the combination of temperature + wind + humidity is more important than temperature alone. The comparison aspect (today vs yesterday) is also crucial since people's bodies acclimatize to recent conditions, making relative changes more important than absolute values.
